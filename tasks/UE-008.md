# UE-008 — Realistic Eurofighter cockpit asset

- Status: in progress (revision 7 HUD and detail refinement)
- Owner: Vision (Blender asset); Milu (scope, references, integration)
- Reviewer: Stark
- Dependencies: baseline measurements, consistent real cockpit references, camera calibration

## Scope

Build a separate cockpit-only asset using the existing Eurofighter as a fit envelope.
User authorized Vision to build, Stark to review, and Milu to test visuals and
performance in Unreal. Use a separate comparison map and asset namespace.
Preserve current aircraft, flight behavior and unrelated UE-007 menu work.
Source files include Maps/HighResEurofighter.blend, Maps/Eurofighter.blend and
Godot/Planes/HighResEurofighter.glb. Blender 2.93 and 3.6 are installed.

## Acceptance criteria

- Updated gate: match the user's frontal and oblique photo landmarks and structural
  forms in corresponding comparison renders, not merely improve the old model.
  The user's rejection of revision 5 supersedes the earlier bounded review pass.

- Record transformed dimensions, units, pilot eye and attachment coordinates;
  existing mesh dimensions are not verified manufacturer cockpit measurements.
- Choose a consistent single-seat cockpit reference set before detailed modeling.
- Match canopy/HUD silhouette and instrument layout before fine surface details.
- Author readable panel labels and physically plausible material roughness;
  use geometry for visible depth and baked maps for small surface detail.
- Deliver editable Blender source and an Unreal-compatible cockpit export.
- Inspect matched normal-game before/after pilot renders and all three camera
  views in UE 5.8.4; retain input/menu behavior.
- Record triangles, material sections and texture memory; measure runtime frame
  time at 3440x1440 on the RTX 4060 Ti before claiming acceptable performance.
- Stark independently reviews rendered results; unresolved checks remain explicit.

### Correction review decisions

The first revision-6 drafts failed review: floating shoulder blocks, open legwell
gaps, sparse console surfaces, and a long empty bridge between the pedestal and
stick. Camera fitting alone could not reconcile the frame and instrument cluster;
the numerical fits were approximate diagnostics, not calibrated photogrammetry.
Vision raised the instrument/HUD cluster 100mm, reshaped the bow to narrow at its
lower attachments, closed the bulkhead, and retained a compact stepped HUD.
Stark's third-draft review narrowed remaining work to the lower layout, enclosure
gaps and control density. Photo cameras must be described as estimated inspection
views; no image-space ratio is treated as a surveyed physical dimension.
