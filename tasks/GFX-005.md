# GFX-005 — High-altitude ocean and cloudscape

- Status: in review
- Owner: Vision
- Reviewer: Stark
- Dependencies: cockpit calibration in GFX-004

## Scope
Milu authorizes an aerial ocean/cloud composition inspired by the user's still:
start at 1,800 m offshore (2,500, 1,800, 12,000), retain the calibrated cockpit as default, enable a broad ocean
with animated wave normals and sky reflection, and add separated volumetric
cloud banks below and above the aircraft. Use only original code-native assets.
The low-poly demonstration city is hidden for this ocean scene; its asset and
transform remain available. Preserve Brackley map assets and all flight dynamics.

## Acceptance criteria
- Startup position is configurable and defaults above the lower cloud bank.
- Cockpit camera remains calibrated/current; both cameras can see the horizon.
- Water is visible through cloud gaps and no ocean edge is visible in the capture.
- Clouds have world-space volume, soft density transitions, illuminated tops and
  darker undersides rather than flat discs or a noisy full-screen overlay.
- Real GPU capture must be reviewed by Stark for composition, cloud layering,
  canopy intersections, readability, horizon continuity and visible artifacts.
- Record visual limitations and do not infer performance from a still.

## Evidence and outcome
Milu selected and saved Stark's recommended final camera: cockpit seat
(-2.60, 0.49, 0), 74-degree vertical FOV, four-degree downward pitch. Compared
60/68/74-degree actual captures (the 68-degree trial used the original seat).
Final `artifacts/cockpit.png` and adjacent JSON reflect the saved scene, Godot
4.1.1 Forward+, 1280x720 and 2x MSAA. The last shader revision draws cloud-box
exit faces so the raymarch can remain visible from inside a bank; final default
view rerendered and inspected with no shader compilation errors. An actual
inside-bank flight test remains pending. Existing missing-input and exit
resource warnings persist; flight dynamics are unchanged.

Implemented an exported startup position (no changes to dynamics), 160 km ocean
PlaneMesh, animated analytic wave normals with dielectric sky reflection, 60 km
camera far planes, and two world-space raymarched cloud volumes. Each cloud
pixel samples up to 48 density steps with three towards-sun samples for shading;
large-scale masks and variable top heights break coverage into banks. The upper
bank is intentionally sparse. Existing atmospheric haze reduced for long views.
No new textures, downloads or dependencies. Camera seat/FOV calibration retained.

First actual Godot 4.1.1 GPU capture exposed unsupported `fog_disabled` shader
mode; removed it. Next capture compiled but Stark and Vision found excessive
white coverage and a visible map edge. Revised bank masks, density/extinction,
shading and offshore location. Second rendered view showed separated banks and
visible water; ocean had overly coherent stripes. Reduced swell slopes and albedo
variation, added phase warping and per-wave derivative filtering, faded subpixel
ripples by distance and increased roughness. Increased cloud steps from 32 to 48
to reduce fixed-step contour bands. Vision inspected the subsequent actual GPU
capture (`artifacts/cockpit.png`, 1280x720, cockpit FOV 68): strong ocean comb
stripes are removed, cloud gaps expose blue water and the previous land edge is
absent. Water now reads relatively calm; cloud shapes remain soft and stretched,
with a pale horizon band. These are prototype limitations, not photorealistic
completion. Stark's final review and Milu's camera experiments remain pending. Milu owns
capture launch and board. `git diff --check` passed on the implementation.

Known limitations: clouds do not cast geometry shadows on the ocean and are not
included in its sky-only reflection. Cloud raymarch does not sample scene depth,
so volumes intersecting other geometry require further testing. Finite ocean
and cloud extents are tuned for this startup view, not an endless-world system.
Camera near/far ratio remains large to preserve cockpit surfaces while viewing
the horizon; distant depth precision and motion need review. No performance
claim is made. Source built-ins checked against the [Godot 4.1 spatial shader
reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.1/tutorials/shaders/shader_reference/spatial_shader.html).

## Stark final rendered review — 2026-10-31

Verdict: the bounded high-altitude ocean/cloud first pass is visually delivered
in the reviewed cockpit stills. Full realism and motion acceptance remain open;
keep this task in review rather than imply photorealistic completion.

Inspected actual `artifacts/cockpit-fov68.png`, `cockpit-fov60.png` and
`cockpit-fov74.png` and the 74-degree capture metadata. All are 1280x720 GPU
views of the actual flight scene. Metadata identifies Godot 4.1.1, cockpit camera,
near 0.01, far 60000, and camera elevation approximately 1800.49 for the 74 view.
These are still comparisons; wind/time and small random flight disturbances
mean they are not pixel-identical controlled benchmarks.

Recommended composition: **FOV 74, cockpit parent seat (-2.60, 0.49, 0)**,
retaining the four-degree downward calibration. It opens the sky/water view and
places the smaller instrument panel lower in the frame, closer to the reference's
balance of cockpit and exterior. The 60-degree version overfills the view with
panel and canopy. The 68-degree original seat (-2.584, 0.46, 0) has a more
dominant canopy arch. Since the seat also changes, these comparisons do not
isolate FOV alone. The recommended frame remains clearly inside the cockpit;
no obvious near-plane cut or canopy/cloud overlap is visible in that still.

Observed improvements:
- High-flight composition replaces the former tower-dominated street-level view.
- Broad blue water openings separate lower cloudbanks; water is distinguishable
  and no finite ocean edge or prior foreground land chunk is visible here.
- Lower banks show soft volume and shaded sides beneath lighter surfaces.
- The earlier dense periodic water comb stripes are absent in all three reviewed
  final captures. Water reads calm rather than unnaturally corrugated.
- Cockpit silhouette, HUD glass and large instrument screens remain identifiable.

Remaining visible realism gaps:
- Clouds remain broad, soft and stretched rather than the reference's crisp
  rounded cumulus masses; upper wisps are relatively flat.
- A pale straight horizon band limits atmospheric depth and horizon coherence.
- Fine instrument labels remain too soft/dark to read at 1280x720. This is
  existing cockpit asset/detail quality, not resolved by changing FOV.
- Canopy reflections, detailed surface variation and cloud shadows on water
  are absent or not convincingly established in these views.

Future acceptance: inspect external view, camera switching/look, ascent/descent
through cloud volumes, scene-depth intersections, horizon movement and finite
world boundaries. Measure frame times before judging the cost of the raymarch.
The lack of scene-depth termination, cloud water reflections/shadows and the
large camera near/far ratio remain explicit implementation limitations. The
missing YawLeft action and exit resource warnings also prevent an error-free
runtime claim. None of these stills establishes performance or motion stability.
