# GFX-004 — Default cockpit framing and background capture

- Status: done
- Owner: Vision / Milu
- Reviewer: Stark
- Dependencies: current flight scene and local Godot 4.1.1 GPU renderer

## Scope
Start in the cockpit and preserve calibrated camera rotation during controller
look. Tune framing toward the user's reference. Provide a separate, hidden
render capture path so reviews do not require desktop clicks or editor control.

## Acceptance criteria
- Cockpit selected in scene and on startup; switching and look remain available.
- Capture uses the actual flight scene and assets, not generated imagery.
- Renderer saves a 1280x720 PNG and records camera/render metadata.
- Background capture exits automatically and leaves the editor alone.
- Inspect the resulting cockpit framing and record remaining differences.

## Evidence and outcome
Initial cockpit startup verified through an interactive game run. Corrected final
seat position (-2.584, 0.46, 0), 68-degree vertical FOV, and 4-degree downward
pitch verified in actual offscreen captures. Default cockpit selection is checked
by the capture script; switching/look code remains present, with calibrated base
rotations preserved. Interactive switching/look was not exercised in this pass.

Repeatable capture: `tools/Render-Cockpit.ps1` launches a separate offscreen,
unfocusable Godot process. Real Flight.tscn gameplay runs for eight frames, then
freezes for lighting settling. Output: `artifacts/cockpit.png` and
`artifacts/cockpit.json`, 1280x720, Godot 4.1.1 Forward+, RTX 4060 Ti, 2x MSAA.
Renderer exited successfully in approximately 2.5 seconds without desktop input.
Logs are isolated alongside the PNG. This still uses the local GPU; no remote
rendering environment is available. Minimizing was tested and timed out; keep
the renderer unfocusable and offscreen without minimizing it.

Milu accepted Stark's still-image framing review. The capture is not a performance
benchmark or a claim of reference-level realism. Existing missing YawLeft input
and exit resource warnings remain in logs; a successful PNG is not a clean-log
gameplay validation. No flight dynamics were changed.

## Stark reviewer outcome — 2026-10-31

Verdict: pass for default cockpit framing and actual-scene still capture. This
is not a pass for photorealism, interactive camera regression testing or clean
runtime logs. Inspected `artifacts/cockpit.png`, `artifacts/cockpit.json`, the
camera startup/look code and the offscreen capture implementation.

Evidence: 1280x720, Godot 4.1.1 stable, Forward+, Vulkan, RTX 4060 Ti, 2x MSAA;
`Plane/CameraCockpit/Camera3D_cockpit`, FOV 68, near 0.01, far 4000. The PNG
clearly shows a centered cockpit, canopy arch, forward HUD glass and instrument
panel with a usable forward view. This satisfies the composition direction of
the reference without pretending to reproduce its aircraft or airborne setting.
No obvious near-plane cut through the cockpit is visible in this frame.

Source review: `idxCamera = 1` agrees with scene `current = true`, and `_ready()`
explicitly selects that camera. Capturing each camera's initial rotation and
adding controller look offsets retains the cockpit's four-degree downward
calibration; camera cycling remains present. Controller switching/look after
startup still requires an interactive check; a still does not establish it.

Observed visual limitations, for subsequent tasks:
- Cockpit textures and instruments appear soft; fine labels are not readable
  at this output size. The dark panel lacks the reference's convincing surface
  variation and dense crisp detail.
- The sky is a smooth gradient with no cloud layers. The setting is low over
  a city at roughly 10 units altitude rather than the reference's high flight.
- The ground texture is stretched/soft and large surrounding towers dominate
  the forward view. World scale and scenery placement warrant separate review.
- The canopy arch is prominent, while convincing glass reflections and subtle
  canopy material cues are absent from this frame.
- No obvious heavy SSAO halo appears in this still, but motion stability,
  camera clipping through turns and performance remain unmeasured.

Capture review: the tool loads the actual Flight scene in a GPU SubViewport and
checks its selected camera, image dimensions and metadata. It uses an offscreen
unfocusable root and a separate process with timeout; it does not automate the
editor or desktop. Eight gameplay frames then sixteen frozen settling frames
make this a review still, not a deterministic motion benchmark. Existing
`YawLeft` missing-action errors and exit resource/leak warnings remain in the
log; the successful PNG must not be described as an error-free runtime run.

No framing correction is required for this bounded task. Milu should record
interactive switching/look and warning cleanup separately from this still pass.
