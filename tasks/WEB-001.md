# WEB-001 — Separate Three.js flight visual prototype

- Status: in review
- Owners: Milu (application/cockpit/controls), Vision (world/atmosphere)
- Reviewer: Stark

## Scope agreed before implementation
Create a separate `threejs-prototype` without modifying the Godot game. Build a
pilot's cockpit view over warm low-sun countryside/coastland, detailed patchwork
terrain, rivers/water and scattered volumetric clouds. Use original procedural
code and existing project assets only; no new external media or image generation.
Milu owns application, renderer, controls, UI and browser capture/testing.
Vision owns `src/world.js` and related world shader modules only.

## World interface
`createWorld(scene, renderer)` returns `update(dt, timeSeconds, cameraPosition)`,
`setWeather(preset)`, `setCloudCover(value)`, `setQuality(value)` and `dispose()`.
World forward is -Z, up is +Y; initial
camera is approximately (0, 1800, 3000), sunlight comes from (-0.6, 0.3, -0.6).

## Acceptance criteria
- Browser prototype remains isolated from all Godot files and controls.
- Aerial landscape has height, field/woodland variation, roads and a river/coast.
- Water and sky share coherent atmospheric colour; finite edges are hidden.
- Clouds have volumetric shading and irregular structure, not repeated spheres.
- Actual browser screenshots are reviewed by Stark; visual and performance
  claims require corresponding evidence.

## Evidence and outcome
Functional prototype completed and approved by Stark as a bounded visual study.
Keep this task in review for the stricter visual criteria: natural detailed
cumulus and general finite-world edge handling remain partial.

Final implementation is isolated in `threejs-prototype/`, with pinned Three.js
0.187.0, Vite 7.2.2 and Playwright 1.62.1. Default Gripen eye (0, 0.65, -5.03),
FOV 68, pitch -4 degrees, near/far 0.1/100000. A forward FOV74 camera was rendered
and rejected because the arch dominated the view. The Eurofighter remains an
alternate local asset. No Godot files were edited for this request.

The Gripen's legacy material extension is converted approximately at runtime;
glass, mirror and trim adjustments restore the inside view. Disconnected display
components receive corrected planar UVs and generated navigation/system screens.
These are illustrative prototype displays, not authentic avionics. Embedded
Gripen attribution (ngkoret, CC BY 4.0) and the repository Eurofighter's separate
noncommercial/share-alike provenance are recorded in `public/credits.html`.

The world contains 180 km rolling terrain with warped agricultural parcels,
woodland ridges, filtered soil/canopy detail, roads, a winding river and coast,
plus a broad reflective ocean. A smooth sky gradient and warm low sun share
atmospheric colours with water/terrain. Up to twelve cloud banks use 80 bounded
ray steps in High or 40 in Balanced. Weather, cover and quality controls alter
the actual scene. Soft cloud shadows project active bank centres onto an
approximate terrain datum; these are not exact volumetric shadows.

Actual renders informed camera, glass, texture, terrain and atmosphere revisions.
An opaque inherited exhaust effect was hidden after exterior review. Reversed
depth (when the browser supports EXT_clip_control), a larger near plane and a
reduced far plane removed the observed water-through-terrain artifact in the
tested mobile view. Other hardware uses conventional depth and remains a limit.

Validation: production build passed; it reports a nonblocking >500 kB Three.js
bundle warning. Nineteen headless Chrome checks passed, including HTTP 200, both
models, camera modes/look, real cloud draw-call reduction at zero cover, settings,
presets, advancing flight, reset, clean view and mobile layout. A keyboard-focus
bug found by the tests was corrected. Final capture is 1600x1000 with no browser
errors or warnings. Pilot, exterior and mobile screenshots were inspected.

Evidence: `threejs-prototype/artifacts/prototype.png` and adjacent JSON,
`exterior.png`, `mobile.png`, `verification.json`; launch/capture instructions in
the prototype README. The renderer's counters and instantaneous FPS display are
not performance benchmarks. Cloud depth intersections, temporal stability,
endless-world behaviour, full simulation and racing rules are not complete.

## Stark final independent review — 2026-11-01

Verdict: approve the bounded, separate browser prototype as a functional visual
study. The strict natural-cumulus/photorealism objectives remain partial; do not
interpret this approval as meeting every realism criterion. No blocking issue
was found in the reviewed final pilot, exterior or mobile views.

Actual evidence: `threejs-prototype/artifacts/prototype.png` (1600x1000),
`exterior.png`, `mobile.png`, their supporting metadata, and
`verification.json`. Final pilot metadata identifies loaded Gripen, Golden
preset, High quality, FOV 68, seat (0, 0.65, -5.03), four-degree downward pitch,
altitude 1800, reversed depth, and empty browser error/warning arrays. The
verification artifact records nineteen passing checks, including camera modes,
FOV/exposure/quality/cover controls, presets, drag/recenter, flight advance/reset,
clean view, mobile fit and alternate aircraft loading. Those checks are Milu's
automated evidence; Stark independently inspected the rendered images and read
the results rather than manually exercising all controls.

Observed strengths:
- The detailed existing Gripen now renders from inside the cockpit. Authored
  gauges, labels and panel geometry give substantially stronger cockpit detail
  than the earlier Eurofighter view. Legacy material conversion preserves its
  texture art, while selective trim/glass treatment makes the exterior visible.
- Default seat/FOV is preferable to the tested forward-seat/FOV74 variant, whose
  canopy arch overfills the view. The final view balances instruments and terrain
  without obvious near-plane cuts in the reviewed still.
- The three generated display surfaces map upright and fill their screens;
  the prior rotated/cropped atlas artifact is resolved. They are illustrative
  prototype displays, not authentic operational avionics.
- Warm light at the left and deeper blue away from the sun improve separation
  over the first washed scene. Irregular agricultural parcels, wooded ridges,
  winding river and receding hills create readable landscape depth.
- Broad soft projected shadow regions are visible on terrain and add variation.
  These are approximate bank projections, not exact volumetric cloud shadows.
- Exterior view presents the aircraft without the former flame spike. Mobile
  view keeps toolbar and canvas within the narrow viewport; the cockpit side
  framing is naturally cropped rather than breaking the layout.

Remaining visual limitations:
- Clouds still repeat soft paired-lobe motifs and outlined edges. They have
  volume/shading but do not yet reach natural detailed cumulus structure.
- Terrain remains visibly procedural, with broad flat crop colours and schematic
  roads/river. Distant coast strips remain a follow-up observation rather than
  a proven general finite-world boundary solution.
- Mirrors use static reflective-looking surfaces; canopy reflection and light
  transport are approximations. Exterior aircraft shading is very pale/white,
  with limited surface response visible in the reviewed frame.
- The inherited cockpit includes simplified/missing material details; modern
  physical shading approximates its legacy specular/glossiness BRDF. The project
  has not validated true aircraft dimensions, pilot eye point or avionics.
- Opaque-depth termination for clouds, intersections during turns, temporal
  stability and finite world limits still need broader motion tests.

Performance boundary: screenshots and renderer counters do not establish a
frame-time budget. The displayed instantaneous FPS values differ between views;
neither they nor headless browser checks constitute a representative hardware
benchmark. No performance or photorealism completion claim is approved.
