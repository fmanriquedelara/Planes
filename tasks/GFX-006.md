# GFX-006 — Cumulus shape, daylight and cockpit view

- Status: done
- Owner: Vision
- Reviewer: Stark

## Scope agreed before implementation
Improve the existing ocean-flight prototype's biggest perceptual gaps: replace
stretched cloud sheets with cellular cumulus clusters and vertical relief, tune
daylight/sky/exposure for a crisp exterior and readable shaded cockpit, and
evaluate cockpit field of view with actual renders. Preserve inside-cockpit
startup, user work, all flight dynamics and controls. No downloads, imagegen or
aircraft replacement. Milu owns render tooling and launch.

## Acceptance criteria
- Rounded cloud lobes, localized self-shadowing and blue-water gaps are visible.
- Cloud march remains bounded and supports views from within the volume.
- Sample opaque scene depth to terminate rays at foreground geometry.
- Horizon cloud coverage fades continuously; avoid a straight opaque deck edge.
- Sun direction, cloud shading, sky and haze use a coherent daylight palette.
- Rendered comparison evaluates cockpit readability and FOV without claiming
  that FOV changes can restore detail absent from the aircraft textures.
- Record unresolved visual, intersection, motion and performance limits.

## Evidence and outcome
Accepted by Milu following Stark's independent review of actual GPU renders.
Baseline: `artifacts/gfx006-before-1080.png`; final saved-scene render:
`artifacts/gfx006-final.png`. Both are 1920x1080, Godot 4.1.1 Forward+ with 2x MSAA.
Adjacent JSON records the actual camera and renderer. Shader time and the changed
flight altitude mean these are visual comparisons, not bit-identical benchmarks.

Final changes:
- Default cockpit FOV 70 degrees, seat (-2.60, 0.49, 0), downward pitch 4 degrees,
  near plane 0.05 and far plane 60000. Compared with 74 degrees and a closer,
  higher seat; that seat exposed an unconvincing aircraft surface, so it was
  rejected. The smaller near/far ratio improves depth precision by construction;
  no claim of solving all distant depth artifacts is made.
- Configurable starting altitude 2200 m; flight dynamics and controls unchanged.
- Warm directional sunlight at rotation (-38, 60, 0), energy 2.4, sky ambient
  0.85, filmic exposure 1.05. Retains shaded cockpit contrast and reveals frame
  and panel surface relief. Hides the arbitrary debug `w` overlay.
- Custom daylight sky with a smooth angular gradient and explicit colour
  bindings shared by ocean and clouds. Ocean fades into that colour at 35–55 km;
  global fog is disabled for this ocean composition to avoid double application.
- Cloud clusters use varied cellular lobes, structural warping, erosion,
  self-shadow samples and 96 bounded, near-biased integration steps. Density
  filtering reduces sampling bands and removes the earlier unaccumulated grain.
  Distant contribution fades over 6.5–16 km. Upper coverage is deliberately sparse.
- Clouds draw exit faces and terminate at opaque scene depth, keeping cockpit
  surfaces in front. Cloud haze is integrated at sample distances instead of
  applying environment fog at the distant bounding-box face.
- Brackley and demonstration city assets remain intact but are hidden in the
  ocean composition, avoiding finite map slabs in the open-water view.

Checks: all final shaders compiled in actual offscreen GPU runs; `git diff
--check` passed. Test captures `gfx006-inside-lower.png` and
`gfx006-inside-upper.png` place the aircraft inside the cloud bounding volumes
at 850 m and 4200 m. They preserve the cockpit and visible cloud field. These
are isolated stills, not continuous traversal or guaranteed dense-cloud immersion.

Limitations: remaining soft cloud detail/contours, simplified sky and flat distant
ocean, no cloud shadows or cloud reflections on water, and atlas markings that
already lack crisp source detail. Transparent glass is absent from opaque-depth
sampling. Motion, frame times, complete geometry intersections, dynamic look and
camera switching remain unverified in this pass. Existing missing `YawLeft` input
and exit resource warnings persist; captures are not clean-log gameplay checks.

The capture tool now supports 720p/1080p and explicit inside-volume test views;
these overrides never modify the saved scene. Scene-depth reconstruction follows
the [Godot 4.1 depth-texture documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.1/tutorials/shaders/advanced_postprocessing.html).

## Stark final reviewer outcome — 2026-10-31

Verdict: **approve this bounded FOV, daylight and cloud improvement**. It produces
observable gains over the baseline; full photorealism is a future objective, not
a condition for accepting this scoped delivery. No further blocking source or
still-image correction is requested for this pass.

Evidence inspected: `artifacts/gfx006-before-1080.png` and
`artifacts/gfx006-final.png` (both 1920x1080), plus actual GPU
`gfx006-inside-lower.png` and `gfx006-inside-upper.png` (1280x720) and their JSON
metadata. Final metadata records Godot 4.1.1, Forward+, Vulkan, RTX 4060 Ti,
2x MSAA, seed 604, cockpit camera, FOV 70, near 0.05, far 60000 and camera height
approximately 2200.50. Final seat is (-2.60, 0.49, 0), with four-degree downward
calibration. Baseline and final differ in altitude, atmosphere and camera FOV;
this is an overall visual comparison, not an isolated measurement of one setting.

Observed improvements:
- Warm direct lighting now reveals canopy-frame relief, fasteners and cockpit
  surface variation much more clearly than the uniformly shaded baseline.
  Large instruments remain identifiable; the dark cockpit still contrasts with
  the bright exterior.
- Clouds have localized rounded masses, varied silhouettes and shaded portions
  instead of the baseline's long stretched sheets. Open blue-water gaps remain.
- The prominent horizontal integration slices/balloon repetition and static
  salt-grain artifacts seen in intermediate captures are substantially reduced.
- The hard ocean cutoff and bright horizon stripe from intermediate tests are
  absent in the final startup view; sky and distant ocean blend continuously.
- FOV 70 is a useful middle ground: instruments are slightly larger than at 74,
  with sufficient exterior view. No obvious near-plane cuts through the cockpit
  are visible in the reviewed final frame. This is composition approval, not
  proof of eye-position or projection accuracy for a real Eurofighter.

Inside-volume evidence: metadata puts the camera at approximately 850.50 and
4200.50 for the lower/upper diagnostic views. Clouds remain visible from those
positions and do not paint over the opaque instrument panel or canopy frame.
These tests demonstrate rendering from inside each volume's bounds and support
the foreground-depth fix. They do **not** establish that the eye is inside a
region of substantial cloud density: large clear gaps remain visible. Dense
interior immersion, volume boundary transitions, transparent canopy sorting and
arbitrary terrain intersections still need targeted motion/dense-density tests.

Remaining nonblocking limitations:
- Cloud edges and interiors are still soft; small repeated lobes and faint
  right-side contour/striation patterns remain. They lack the reference's
  detailed cumulus structure and rich multiple-scattering appearance.
- The ocean reads as a broad blue surface with limited large-scale reflection
  variation. The sky is a simple gradient; the resulting atmosphere is coherent
  but not a physically validated atmosphere/ocean system.
- Cockpit fine markings remain blurred and mostly unreadable. Inspection of
  the actual upscaled atlas found smeared markings and simplified baked screen
  graphics already in the source. Render resolution and lighting cannot restore
  authentic detail absent from that atlas. Shared 1024-pixel normal/metal-rough
  maps, mip/compression choices and screen projection can also affect clarity.
- Cloud shadows/reflections on water, finite world boundaries, external-camera
  quality and interactive switching/look are not comprehensively verified here.
- These stills do not measure performance, frame-time stability or temporal
  aliasing. Existing missing-YawLeft and exit resource warnings remain separate
  runtime issues; successful captures are not an error-free-run claim.
